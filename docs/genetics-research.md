# MICROCOSM — Genetics Phase Research

v1.0 · 2026-08-28 · Three lenses (biology · game mechanics · cybernetics), then the synthesis the design phase will build on. Sources searched 2026-08-28; the two empirical anchors are the strongest evidence available anywhere for what evolution does in exactly our kind of system.

## 1. Biology — what actually evolves in clonal microbial worlds

**1.1 The Lenski LTEE (the ground truth for asexual evolution).** Twelve initially identical asexual E. coli populations, serially propagated since 1988, now past 80,000 generations. Findings that transfer directly: (a) **Fitness rises in discrete sweeps, not smoothly** — ~30% in the first 2,000 generations, in roughly three ~10% steps, each step a beneficial mutation taking ~250 generations to sweep from rare to majority. Our Observatory's mean-trait channels should show *staircases*, not ramps, and that is a feature: sweeps are visible drama. (b) **Improvement decelerates but never stops** — even a constant environment yields open-ended refinement. (c) **Contingency is real**: the famous citrate innovation arose in one population of twelve after ~30,000 generations — replicate worlds diverge by luck, which our seeded determinism can showcase deliberately (same settings, different seeds, different evolutionary outcomes). (d) **Ecotypes emerge and coexist from trade-offs alone**: a slower-growing acetate-specialist lineage stably coexisted with the glucose specialist for 14,000+ generations — speciation-adjacent structure without sex, geography, or design, purely from metabolic trade-offs. Our world already runs on trade-offs; ecotype splitting is a realistic emergent target, not a scripted feature.

**1.2 Yoshida/Hairston rotifer–algae chemostats (the eco-evolutionary anchor — literally our Drifta–Cilio system).** The landmark result: when algal prey carry genetic variation in a defense-vs-competitiveness trade-off, **the predator-prey cycles themselves transform** — genetically uniform prey give the classic short cycles with quarter-period lag (which our Populations chart shows today, and which I pointed out to you as "the textbook signature"), while evolvable prey produce **long-period antiphase cycles** (predator maxima at prey minima), a pattern described as virtually diagnostic of eco-evolutionary dynamics. The follow-up ("cryptic cycles", 2007, with Lenski as co-author — the two anchors share an author): when defense is effective and cheap, defended and undefended genotype frequencies cycle in counterbalance so that **total prey density stays nearly constant while the genetic composition churns underneath** — trophic interactions become invisible in abundance data alone. For us this is a double gift: it predicts a *specific, falsifiable, chart-visible transformation* of an oscillation we already display, and it justifies the mean-trait channels the recorder has carried since 4.0 — without them, evolution in our world could literally hide.

**1.3 Design-relevant population genetics.** Effective population size decides everything: with Drifta at 200–1,500 and Bacillus near 1,000, selection can act on percent-level fitness differences; with Venator at ~25, **drift dominates** — its traits will random-walk, not adapt, and honest design either accepts that (drift is real biology) or excludes the predator from headline evolution claims. Mutation-selection balance sets standing variation; our existing size-mutation (σ=0.08, 20% of divisions) is already a quantitative-genetics engine awaiting more traits. Heritability in clonal fission is ~1 minus mutation — the retention channel is nearly perfect, which is *why* microbial evolution is fast.

## 2. Game mechanics — how sims make evolution playable, and where they fail

The field splits into two archetypes. **Player-as-editor** (Spore, Thrive): the player redesigns their organism in an editor between rounds; Thrive pairs this with background species evolving via population-dynamics-driven random mutation. Engaging, but the player's own lineage doesn't *evolve* — it's intelligently designed, and the "evolution" label draws criticism accordingly. **Player-as-observer** (Bibites, ALIEN, Avida-style alife): genuine variation-selection runs and the player watches, breeds, or perturbs; the recurring failure mode is *invisibility* — evolution happens in the numbers and the player sees only wiggling creatures, so these sims live or die by their instrumentation (lineage browsers, trait graphs, mutation logs).

Microcosm's position is already decided by its purpose: **observer-with-levers**. The player never edits genomes; they change the *selection environment* (sun, mineral, predation via kill, settings) and read the consequences — which is both the scientifically honest stance and the one our Observatory uniquely supports, because the invisibility problem that sinks observer sims is precisely what Phase 4 was built to solve. Mechanics shortlist from the survey: pigment tied to genotype (drift you can *see* in the world's colors — the cheapest visibility win), mean-trait time series with intervention markers (exists), trait histograms per species (variance made visible — variance is the fuel gauge of evolution), sweep detection as event-feed entries ("a faster Drifta lineage is taking over — 62% and rising"), and lineage/ancestry queries on the specimen card ("generation 214; this line has grown 12% larger than its founder"). Anti-patterns to avoid: XP-like "evolution points" (Spore's shopping), forced speciation theater, and any mechanic where the player selects *outcomes* rather than *pressures*.

## 3. Cybernetics — the second-order lens

**3.1 Requisite variety (Ashby).** Only variety absorbs variety: a population's genetic variance is its *variety reservoir* against environmental disturbance. This reframes the whole phase: the levers inject disturbance; standing variation determines whether the population can answer it. A monoculture facing a dimmed sun has one answer; a varied population has a distribution of answers, and selection amplifies the good ones. The Yoshida result is requisite variety made empirical: the variable prey population absorbs predation pressure that annihilates the clone. The line worth remembering: *diversity is not decoration — it is the system's capacity to respond.*

**3.2 Variation–selection–retention as the universal learning loop (Campbell's evolutionary epistemology; March's exploration/exploitation).** Mutation = exploration; selection = evaluation; heredity = retention. Most learning-system frameworks are this loop wearing a suit. The genetics phase makes the loop *watchable*: mutation rate is the exploration budget, selection pressure is set by the player's levers, and the mean-trait staircase is organizational learning rendered as a chart. Exploitation-only (no mutation) = the Lenski clone = brittle optimum; exploration-only (high mutation) = error catastrophe; the tunable middle is the interesting region.

**3.3 First- and second-order loops.** Ecology is the first-order loop (populations regulating around the tuned equilibria we built); evolution is the second-order loop (the *parameters* of the first loop drifting under selection). Eco-evolutionary dynamics — the Yoshida transformation — is what happens when the two loops run at comparable speeds and couple. And the Observatory is the third loop: the observer watching both, which is where second-order cybernetics (observing systems) stops being a slogan and becomes a product screen. One honest caveat from the same lens: our hand-tuned ecology is a fitness landscape we sculpted; **evolution is an optimizer and will find its exploits** — every stabilizer we tuned (graze floors, interference, escape probabilities) becomes a selection target if made heritable. That is the phase's R1, inherited from cybernetics rather than discovered by crash.

## 4. Synthesis — what the design phase should build (research conclusions, pre-design)

**4.1 Genome = heritable multipliers on existing normalized traits.** No new trait system: a small per-organism vector (initially 3–5 loci per species) multiplying selected `TRAITS` values (speed, kp, escape.p, digestion, size already done), inherited at division with per-locus mutation. `normalizeTraits` (3.0b) and the RNG contract make this a contained change; loci that a species lacks consume no draws.

**4.2 Trade-offs are mandatory, not optional.** Every locus must carry a cost (speed → movement energy, defense → growth, kp → mQm demand), because (a) biology: the Yoshida transformation *requires* the defense-competitiveness trade-off; (b) the LTEE ecotypes emerged from trade-offs; (c) engineering: a free-lunch locus is a runaway exploit against our tuned landscape (R1). The existing physics already prices most of these — the design task is auditing that every heritable knob is priced.

**4.3 The flagship experiment and candidate phase gate: the Yoshida test.** Give Drifta one heritable defense-vs-growth locus. Prediction from the literature, falsifiable in our harness: cycle period lengthens and the Drifta–Cilio phase relationship shifts from quarter-lag toward antiphase, versus the mutation-off control (same seeds — a true controlled evolution experiment, which the real chemostats needed heroic effort to run and we get for free from determinism). If the cryptic regime appears (constant totals, churning genotype shares), the mean-trait and variance channels must reveal it — the Observatory earning its keep on the exact phenomenon it was built for. This is to the genetics phase what K6 retro-narration was to Phase 4.

**4.4 Visibility layer (cheap, high yield):** genotype-tinted pigment within species hue; trait histograms and mean±variance ribbons on a new Data page; sweep/diversity events in the feed ("variation collapsing in Solara — the population is becoming uniform"); ancestry line on the specimen card. **4.5 Scope discipline:** no sex, no explicit speciation mechanics, no horizontal transfer in the first cut — the LTEE shows clonal mutation+selection alone delivers sweeps, ecotypes, contingency, and open-ended change; if ecotype splitting emerges from trade-offs we celebrate it as emergent, we do not script it.

**4.6 Risks, ranked.** R1 evolution-vs-tuning (the optimizer finds exploits; mitigations: priced loci, bounded multiplier ranges, 8-seed long-horizon runs, and the conformance discipline unchanged — mutation-off worlds must stay bit-identical). R2 drift-noise misread as adaptation at small N (mitigate: variance channels + honest per-species claims; Venator excluded from adaptation headlines). R3 invisibility (mitigated by 4.4 — this phase exists because the Observatory does). R4 timescale: sweeps need generations — at Drifta generation ~300–600 ticks, a 10%-advantage sweep ≈ tens of minutes at 1×, comfortable at 16×; trait effect sizes must be chosen so a sweep is visible within a single session.
